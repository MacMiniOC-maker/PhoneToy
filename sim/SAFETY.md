## 2. Loudness

This is the part that matters most for a toddler. The numbers assume a speaker sensitivity of 85 dB at 1 W / 1 m, which is a guess for these parts, so treat them as ±10 dB and measure.

The WHO safe-listening level for children is 75 dB averaged over time.

| Case | Speaker power | Estimated level |
|---|---|---|
| Ringer at the firmware setting, 50 cm away | 30 mW | 76 dB |
| Ringer if the gain were set to maximum, 50 cm away | 3133 mW | 96 dB |
| Earpiece at the firmware setting, loudest possible signal, 5 cm | 1.5 mW | 83 dB |
| Earpiece with no resistor and the gain at maximum, 5 cm | 1566 mW | 113 dB |

Speech averages about 20 dB below the loudest possible signal, so everyday levels are well under these.

Software alone is a weak guard: without a resistor, one wrong number in `config.h` gives the last row. A resistor in series with the earpiece speaker caps the level in hardware, whatever the software does:

## 3. What happens if something is wired wrong

| Mistake | What the model shows | Result | How it is prevented |
|---|---|---|---|
| Button LED has no built-in resistor | 242 mA through the LED | LED burns out; transistor dissipates 93 mW and survives | Test one LED on 5 V first; add 220 Ω if unsure |
| Light wire shorts to 5 V, base resistor 1 kΩ | 284 mA, 1.42 W in the transistor, about 318 °C (limit 150 °C) | **Transistor overheats, hot enough to scorch cardboard** | Normal operation with this resistor: 12.9 mA, 33 mV drop (still fully on) |
| Light wire shorts to 5 V, base resistor 2.2 kΩ | 163 mA, 0.82 W in the transistor, about 198 °C (limit 150 °C) | **Transistor overheats and fails** | Normal operation with this resistor: 12.8 mA, 44 mV drop (still fully on) |
| Light wire shorts to 5 V, base resistor 4.7 kΩ | 90 mA, 0.45 W in the transistor, about 125 °C (limit 150 °C) | Transistor gets hot but survives | Normal operation with this resistor: 12.7 mA, 59 mV drop (still fully on) |
| Transistor in backwards | 6.1 mA through the LED | Light is dim or dark; nothing is damaged | Check the leg diagram |
| Microphone VDD on 5 V instead of 3V3 | 5 V against a 3.63 V limit | **Microphone destroyed** | It is in the wiring table and the lesson |
| 5 V rail shorted to ground, no fuse (charger supplies about 3 A before it cuts out) | 3 A: 387 mW in a jumper wire (about 80 °C), 450 mW in one breadboard contact | **Contact can soften the breadboard plastic; wire gets hot** | **Add the fuse below** |
| 5 V rail shorted to ground, with a 0.5 A resettable fuse (trips near 1 A, then holds a trickle) | 1 A: 43 mW in a jumper wire (about 40 °C), 50 mW in one breadboard contact | Warm for a few seconds, then off | Fuse trips within seconds and resets when unplugged |
| A GPIO output shorted to ground | the pin limits itself to about 40 mA | Usually survives briefly | Unplug before rewiring |
| Speaker output wire touches ground | the amplifier has short-circuit protection | Amp shuts down, recovers | Never ground either speaker wire |

## 4. Protection to build in

These turn a wiring fault from a heat problem into a non-event. None of them is in the firmware; they are parts and construction.

| Protection | Why | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| 0.5 A resettable fuse (PTC polyfuse) between the board's 5V pin and the red rail | Limits a short across the rails to about 1 A for a few seconds instead of 3 A continuously | a few dollars for a pack |
| 4.7 kΩ base resistors | A shorted light wire heats the transistor to about 125 °C instead of past its limit | in the resistor kit |
| 220 Ω in series with the earpiece (only if the handset is opened) | Hardware cap on earpiece volume | in the resistor kit |
| Solder the final circuit onto a board; do not seal a breadboard in the box | Shaking and dropping work breadboard wires loose, and loose wires are what cause shorts | protoboard, a few dollars |
| Certified 5 V USB charger, plugged in out of his reach | The charger is the only part connected to mains, and its own cut-out is the last line of defence | you may own one |
| Keep the electronics off bare cardboard (a plastic or wooden tray inside the box) | Cardboard is the only thing in the build that burns | scrap material |

## 5. Hazards no simulation covers

For a toddler these matter more than anything electrical. Check each by hand, every time the toy is rebuilt.

| Hazard | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Cords** (strangulation) | No cord he can reach is longer than about 30 cm (12 in) when pulled straight. That includes the handset's coiled cord: feed the spare length inside the box and clamp it. The USB power cord runs straight behind furniture. |
| **Small parts** (choking) | Nothing comes off when pulled hard: button caps, photos, speaker, handset parts. A 30 mm button fits in a toddler's mouth. |
| **Access to the inside** | The box is glued or screwed shut. He cannot open it or reach a wire through any gap. |
| **Loudness** | Measured with a sound-meter app at the earpiece and at the ringer, not estimated. 75 dB or less at the ear. |
| **Heat** | Nothing is warm to the touch after 30 minutes plugged in, and again after a long call. |
| **Batteries** | There are none, by design. Do not add any. |
| **Supervision** | This is a homemade device, not a certified toy. He uses it with an adult in the room, and it is unplugged when nobody is watching. |
